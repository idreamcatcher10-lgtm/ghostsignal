The human network

How a 1990s pager idea became an offline QR messenger.

No cell towers. No cloud. No automatic radio mesh. Just people, their phones, and a little bit of ingenuity.
01

Create

Write a short message, choose its urgency and generate a QR. The message is also saved locally.

02

Scan

Another phone scans your code directly from your screen, or imports the GS1 text. You must be nearby.

03

Carry & relay

The receiving phone stores the message and can show a new QR to someone else. Each relay uses the same message ID.

04

Stay in control

Duplicate IDs, expiry timestamps and a six-hop limit help avoid uncontrolled forwarding. There is no background wireless transfer.

Important limitations

This is a hackathon prototype, not a certified emergency communications system. Messages are not encrypted, identities are not authenticated, and delivery depends entirely on people manually relaying codes. Use official emergency services whenever available.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS
Does it really work without the internet?

Yes, after first opening the site while connected and loading the PWA's service worker, it can create, display and import QR packets offline. Scanning availability depends on your browser. Always test by turning on airplane mode.

Can someone forge a sender label?

Yes. Labels are not verified. CRC32 catches accidental data corruption but is not a digital signature or encryption.

Can the message travel between people far apart?

Only if intermediate people physically carry the QR packet and pass it onward. This app does not send messages over Bluetooth, Wi-Fi Direct, cellular, or mesh networking.
